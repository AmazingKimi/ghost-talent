# Competitive Landscape

Public scan date: **2026-09-07**.

This is a directional scan of adjacent public GitHub projects, not a claim that no similar project exists anywhere.

Ghost Talent sits near recruiting sourcing, candidate discovery, and talent-search tooling, but is built around a different primary question.

## Category distinction

Conventional recruiting search asks:

> I need a CUDA engineer. Who matches this role?

Ghost Talent asks:

> In CUDA, Triton, or inference, who is getting stronger faster than their public visibility?

That distinction is the project thesis:

**Recruiting Search → match known candidates to a known role.**

**Emerging Talent Intelligence → discover rising people before they become obvious candidates.**

Ghost Talent operationalizes that idea with Capability, Momentum, Visibility Gap, Evidence Confidence, Ghost Score, Ghost Radar, immutable snapshots, and first-detected history.

## What appears distinct in the reviewed set

Among the public projects reviewed in this scan, we did not find one centered on the same explicit combination of:

1. a **technical frontier** as the search starting point rather than a job description;
2. **Capability + Momentum + Visibility Gap + Evidence Confidence** as separate inspectable dimensions;
3. public OSS/research evidence as the basis for ranking;
4. immutable **First Detected** history;
5. future evaluation through **Breakout Lead Time** and frozen historical benchmarks.

That combination is the category Ghost Talent is attempting to define as **emerging talent intelligence**.

## Why publish this comparison

The purpose is not to claim that recruiting technology is new. It is not.

The purpose is to make the category boundary explicit and falsifiable. If an adjacent project already implements the same research question and historical evaluation model, open an issue with the evidence and this document should be updated.

## Positioning

**Ghost Talent — Open-source emerging talent intelligence.**

**Discover exceptional AI engineers and researchers before they become obvious.**

The long-term proof is not the wording. It is whether historical Ghost Talent snapshots identify later breakout technical people earlier than simple visibility baselines.
